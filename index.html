Events to watch out for in August 2026
RBI MPC Meeting (August 3–5):
The Monetary Policy Committee met early this month, with RBI Governor announcing on August 5 that the repo rate will remain unchanged at 5.25%. The central bank noted that while overall inflation is modest, risks from food and fuel prices persist. With average banking liquidity remaining above ₹1 lakh crore since June, credit markets and bond yields will continue to be heavily influenced by the RBI's data-dependent stance. The RBI also upped its GDP growth estimates by 10bps to 6.7% for the year and reduced inflation estimates by 10bps to 5.0%.
Corporate Earnings Season (Deadline August 14):
The Q1 FY27 earnings season continues at a rapid pace ahead of the SEBI regulatory deadline of August 14 for most listed companies. Early trends show outperformance in the financial and chemical sectors. Investor sentiment may hinge heavily on operating margins, rural recovery commentary, and how companies are managing localized input costs.
Inflation & Macro Data (August 12):
The critical Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation data for July 2026 will be released on August 12. Following the RBI's recent cautious commentary on food prices, this print will be essential for gauging domestic consumption momentum. It will be accompanied by the Index of Industrial Production (IIP) and Wholesale Price Index (WPI) releases, which will highlight output strength across manufacturing and mining.
Tax Compliance & Deadlines (August 31):
While the standard ITR filing deadline concluded on July 31, taxpayers filing belated returns will now face a late fee of up to ₹5,000 under Section 234F. Additionally, August 31 is the official deadline for eligible taxpayers (such as freelancers and small businesses) to file ITR-3 and ITR-4 without an audit requirement.
Political Developments (Monsoon Session):
The ongoing Monsoon Session of Parliament is scheduled to conclude on August 13, 2026,. As the session wraps up, markets will monitor the progression of pending bills, regulatory reforms, and any debates concerning national policies or regional developments.
Global Cues & Index Rebalancing:
In addition to monitoring US Federal Reserve rate expectations and ongoing geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, markets are focused on the MSCI Quarterly Index Review. The review announcement is scheduled for August 12, with adjustments taking effect on September 1. These rebalancing changes may often trigger significant foreign institutional investor (FII) capital flows in and out of domestic equities.

Market View
Domestic equity markets have remained range-bound, with intermittent bouts of volatility driven by evolving global trade developments, geopolitical uncertainties and mixed corporate earnings.
While easing tensions in the Middle East have helped contain crude oil prices and supported global risk sentiment, investors continue to monitor developments on the global trade front and major central bank policy actions.
Lower crude oil prices, a relatively stable Indian Rupee, resilient domestic macroeconomic fundamentals healthy corporate balance sheets and a turnaround in foreign institutional investor (FIIs) flows with overseas investors turning net buyers have provided support to markets. However, any renewed geopolitical tensions, adverse global policy developments or a sharper slowdown in global growth could keep markets volatile in the near term.
Despite these near-term headwinds, improving domestic demand, sustained government capital expenditure, easing financial conditions and India’s structural growth drivers may be expected to support the country’s medium-term economic and earnings outlook.
Following the market correction over the past several months valuations have become more reasonable particularly within the large-cap segment. While near-term earnings may face pressure from elevated input costs, currency weakness and softer global demand the medium- to long-term structural growth drivers for India might remain intact.
Given geopolitical challenges and its implications, long term investments may be invested systematically using the current corrective phase of the market. Fluctuations in commodity prices, supply chain disruptions, currency movements and global growth concerns are near term factors which may delay recovery. Hence a systematic approach to adding allocations might help in potentially lowering the anticipated volatility. The impact of war is visible on select segments; delayed resolution could lead to downgrade in earnings.
Asset allocation in line with the risk appetite of the investor is an important tool to navigate any unanticipated volatility. Accordingly Large Cap & Large Cap oriented diversified strategies along with hybrid funds might appear to be better placed on risk-reward basis, while Mid/Small cap allocation may be considered in a staggered manner through systematic investment with a long-term view.
Gold & Silver Outlook
After a strong five-year rally, gold (near US$4,200/oz) and silver (around US$65/oz) have entered a consolidation phase. The pullback is likely due to firmer inflation data, expectations of higher interest rates and a rotation from defensive assets into equities. However, the structural drivers behind both metals may remain firmly in place.
The continuing Persian Gulf conflict remains an important source of uncertainty. However, markets appear more concerned about its impact on energy supplies, inflation, interest rates, and economic activity than about the geopolitical event itself. Any renewed disruption to shipping or oil supplies could quickly raise inflation expectations and increase market volatility.
For precious metals, a prolonged conflict is not automatically positive. Higher energy prices may push bond yields and the US dollar higher, creating short term pressure on gold and silver. Conversely, weaker growth, falling confidence, or financial instability could strengthen demand for gold as a defensive asset.
Gold experienced significant volatility during the first half of 2026, with a strong rally followed by a sharp correction and subsequent recovery. This suggests that investors continue to view major declines as potential accumulation opportunities. However, gold may remain highly sensitive to real interest rates, Federal Reserve policy, and movements in the US dollar.
The tactical outlook for gold is potentially neutral to moderately bullish, while the longer term structural outlook remains constructive. Gold might continue to benefit from elevated sovereign debt, reserve diversification, geopolitical fragmentation, and demand for assets that are not directly linked to government credit risk. Central bank purchases remain important sources of support.
Silver also retains a constructive longer term outlook but may considerably be more volatile than gold. Its dual role as a monetary and industrial metal makes it sensitive to both investor sentiment and global economic activity.
The silver market may remain in deficit during 2026, while physical investment demand may provide support. However, industrial demand faces pressure from reduced silver usage and substitution in photovoltaic manufacturing. Demand from data centres, artificial intelligence, automotive electronics, and electrical infrastructure may partly offset this weakness.
The preferred approach for the remainder of 2026 is gradual accumulation rather than chasing strong rallies.
Key indicators to monitor include US real interest rates, the US dollar, oil prices, central bank policy, investment flows, and developments affecting energy supplies in the Persian Gulf. For silver, industrial demand, inventories, physical investment, and the gold to silver ratio are also important.
