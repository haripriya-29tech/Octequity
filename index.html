Output and new orders continued to expand, but both grew at their slowest rates since August 2021 amid softer demand and challenging market conditions. Export orders also increased, although growth in international sales eased from July.

Market View
Earnings recovery seems gaining potential momentum, with Q1 results reflecting improved revenue growth on the back of a low base from last year. Demand indicators are improving across key sectors. Auto volumes, consumer staples demand, and credit growth trends in banks have all shown signs of recovery, pointing to a broad-based improvement in economic activity.
Policy support may be beginning to yield results. Last year's GST and income tax reductions appear to be supporting consumption and business activity, helping strengthen growth momentum potential.
The upcoming festive season may be a key monitorable. A continuation of current demand trends could further improve confidence in earnings growth, an element that has been lacking over the last 6-8 quarters amid tariff uncertainties, elevated energy costs, and global disruptions.
Valuations have become more reasonable. After nearly two years of largely range-bound market performance, markets appear to be favourably priced from a long term point of view.
India's external position has strengthened, with ~ USD 136 billion raised through foreign currency bond issuances. This may help moderate currency depreciation pressures and support a more stable environment for foreign capital flows.
Foreign investor positioning offers room for improvement. Foreign ownership in Indian equities has dropped below 17%, creating scope for potential inflows if earnings visibility continues to improve.
Domestic flows remain resilient, while recent improvement in FII participation might be an encouraging sign. Foreign demand is gradually absorbing the strong supply of equity issuance.
Key risks remain, including the prolonged Middle East crisis, high bond yields and continued equity supply from large IPOs and OFS transactions.
Overall, improving earnings, supportive policy measures, attractive valuations, stable domestic flows, may provide a favourable backdrop for medium-to long term investors.
Asset allocation in line with the risk appetite of the investor is an important tool to navigate any unanticipated volatility. Accordingly Large Cap & Large Cap oriented diversified strategies along with hybrid funds might appear to be better placed on risk-reward basis, while Mid/Small cap allocation may be considered in a staggered manner through systematic investment with a long-term view.
Gold & Silver Outlook
Precious metals continue to navigate changing expectations around monetary policy, real yields and the US dollar. While these remain important near-term influences, recent developments suggest that the structural role of gold in portfolios continues to broaden beyond the traditional rates-and-dollar framework.
A notable development has been the return of institutional investment demand. It has been recently reported that large global asset managers had either rebuilt gold positions following the correction or maintained their existing allocations. The common thread was less about short-term price direction and more about gold's usefulness as a liquid portfolio hedge and diversifier amid macroeconomic, fiscal and geopolitical uncertainty.
This has coincided with renewed flows into gold ETFs after several months of redemptions. ETF participation may particularly be relevant in precious metals because investment flows may reinforce existing market trends, while providing an additional source of demand alongside physical consumers and the official sector.
Central-bank demand remains another important structural pillar. Official-sector purchases strengthened again in the second quarter, with World Gold Council data showing net purchases of 289 tonnes — the highest recorded for a second quarter of CY 2026. Reserve diversification, geopolitical fragmentation and concerns around fiscal sustainability might continue to encourage a gradual reassessment of gold's role within global reserves.
In the past, high real yields^ would have been a negative for gold. However, sustained central-bank and physical demand have helped reduce this sensitivity during the current cycle. Similarly, gold's recent correlation with equities has moderated, reinforcing the relevance of its diversification characteristics within multi-asset portfolios.
The US dollar and real yields^ may remain the principal near-term counterweights. A sustained appreciation in the dollar or materially higher real rates could periodically weigh on potential investment demand. However, concerns around fiscal credibility, reserve diversification and the durability of the dollar's store-of-value characteristics have increasingly entered the institutional asset-allocation debate. Even without any change in the dollar's dominant reserve-currency status, relatively modest diversification at the margin can be meaningful for the gold market.
Silver continues to share many of gold's monetary and investment drivers while retaining a significant industrial component. ETF flows and gold-market direction remain important, but manufacturing activity, solar demand, electrification, recycling and technological substitution introduce additional cyclical influences and contribute to higher volatility. Economic activity in China and USA may have an impact on industrial commodities. A strong US economy potentially supports silver demand, while China's weak economy may weigh on it. The two economies relatively offset each other, making the overall impact on silver prices broadly neutral. . Any material changes in these two economies may have a material impact on industrial metals, including silver.
Overall, near-term volatility in precious metals might be expected as markets respond to monetary policy and currency and bond movements. At the same time, central-bank diversification, renewed institutional participation, ETF demand and gold's evolving role as a portfolio hedge potentially suggest that the longer-term structural case may continue to warrant investor attention.
